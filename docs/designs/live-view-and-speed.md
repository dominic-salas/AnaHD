# Design: See the clean while it renders, and render it faster

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-28
Branch: main
Repo: dominic-salas/AnaHD
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

Cleaning a 3-minute flight takes about 8.4 minutes (README: 21.9 fps on an RTX 4070 Ti;
measured today on a 30 s DVR clip: 18.6 fps end to end). For that whole time the app shows
numbers only: each card reads `42%  19.4 fps  1:10 left` (`app/window.py:911`) and the footer
has a 220 px bar. The numbers are accurate (`app/eta.py` is careful), but there is nothing to
look at, and nothing to learn: the first time the user sees their settings *in motion* is
when the whole file is done. Settings, cuts and output choices are locked while it works
(`window.py:1106-1109`).

The owner wants both halves: show the clean as it happens, and make it take less time.

## What Makes This Cool

You press Clean and watch your own flight come back: the frame the GPU just cleaned, wiped
against the raw DVR frame, refreshing as it goes. Breakup bands vanish in front of you. If
the look is wrong at minute one, you stop at minute one. And the wait gets somewhat shorter: a measured
speed-up from giving the GPU back its idle time (see "Part 2, revised").

## Constraints

- Windows desktop app (PySide6), one worker process per clip over a `multiprocessing`
  queue (`app/worker.py`), polled every 200 ms.
- The GPU is the bottleneck: ~88% busy during a render, CPU ~25% (measured 2026-10-28).
  Anything added to the worker must not cost GPU time worth measuring.
- The look is approved (`tests/data/approved_look.json`). Speed work may not change it
  beyond the existing test tolerances.
- Solo project, shipped through the existing installer and release flow (`docs/RELEASING.md`).

## Measurements behind this design (RTX 4070 Ti, `hdz_30s.mp4`, 1800 frames, 2026-10-28)

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| End to end, defaults, x264 `slow` CRF 12 | 18.6 fps (97 s) |
| Same with `--nvenc` | 19.3 fps (93 s): the encoder is not the bottleneck |
| GPU cleaning alone, repair only (`tools/learned_bench.py`) | 31.0 ms/frame |
| GPU cleaning alone, + Learned v6 U-Net | 38.1 ms/frame: the network costs ~7 ms |
| GPU utilisation mid-render | 85-92% |
| CPU utilisation mid-render | ~25% of 24 threads |

Conclusion: alignment and repair (RAFT-small to six neighbours per frame, LK refine at full
size, breakup detection) is the largest single cost, and the GPU has no idle time to give.

**Baseline used for every estimate below (one source):** the README's 3-minute flight,
11,032 frames in 503 s at default settings. Less ~9 s of start-up (`speed.py` START_S), that
is 494 s of steady work = 22.3 fps = **44.8 ms/frame**, and today's time is **8.4 min**. (The 30 s
clip's 18.6 fps carries the same ~9 s start-up over only 1,800 frames, so it understates
steady speed.) The bench's 38.1 ms covers cleaning only; the other ~7 ms of the 44.8 is the
remaster/look stage, colour conversion, upload and download, which is not profiled yet.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The pain is no picture, not missing numbers; the progress figures stay as they are.
2. The live view shows the cleaned frame against its raw frame, refreshed every 1-2 s, at
   no measurable cost, because the frames are already on the GPU.
3. Watching is only useful if you can act on it: Stop stays one click from the live view.
4. Speed must come from less GPU work per frame. A faster encoder, parallel clips or
   TensorRT for the U-Net (7 ms) cannot move the number much.
5. A speed-up ships only if the look stays within the current test tolerances.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: live view + cheap tuning** (fewer RAFT iterations, narrower radius): S effort,
  ~1.1-1.2x. Not chosen alone: too little speed for "make it faster". Its tuning steps
  are kept as the first, cheapest experiments in B.
- **B: live view + flow reuse behind a quality gate**: chosen (below).
- **C: B plus a fragmented partial MP4 playable while rendering**: deferred. Most moving
  parts (growing-file playback in Qt, `verify()` and the frame-count check, the final remux);
  revisit once B ships.

## Recommended Approach: B

Two independent pieces, shipped in this order.

### Part 1: the live view (ships first, no risk to the look)

**Worker side** (`gpu/pipeline.py` `enhance`, `app/worker.py` `work`):
- `enhance` takes an optional `snapshot(raw_bgr, clean_bgr, index)` callback, beside
  `progress`. At most every `SNAPSHOT_S = 1.5` seconds (the first frame always), the
  writer thread takes the frame it just copied to pinned memory (8-bit I420), converts it to
  BGR and resizes it to 640 px wide on the CPU, and pairs it with the matching raw frame
  (amendment 1 and the factual corrections in the review section).
- Matching frames: the remaster stage can hold frames back (`stage.push` / `flush`), so the
  raw frame is kept in a small ring keyed by frame index, and the snapshot is taken on the
  writer side, when the index is known. If that turns out awkward, snapshot the cleaned
  planes before the remaster stage and say so in the caption ("before the look").
- The worker JPEG-encodes both (`cv2.imencode`, quality 85, ~60 KB each) and puts
  `("frame", before_jpg, after_jpg, index, total)` on the queue. Only the newest is
  kept, as already done with `"progress"`.
- Before the first progress message, the worker sends `("stage", text)` for each start-up
  step: "Counting frames", "Measuring the picture area and OSD", "Loading the models",
  "Reading the OSD" (redraw only). The card shows it instead of "Starting...".

**Window side** (`app/window.py`, `app/worker.py` `Runner`):
- `Runner` gets `frame = Signal(object, bytes, bytes, int, int)` and `stage = Signal(object, str)`.
- While a clip is CLEANING, and it is the selected clip, the preview stage
  (`self._stage`) shows `self._frame_wipe` with `show_pair(before, after)` from each
  snapshot. The heading changes from "Preview · Paused: before / after..." to
  "Cleaning · live: recording / AnaHD; drag the line", with a caption "Frame 4,212 of 11,032 ·
  1:10 into the cleaned clip" (amendment 6). Selecting another clip shows it under the "no GPU previews while a
  clean runs" rule below (its decoded recording frame, no remaster); reselecting the
  cleaning clip shows the latest snapshot at once.
- **Lifecycle of the live view:**
  - On Clean, the first clip to be cleaned is selected, so the live view is what you see
    (pressing Clean is the request to watch).
  - When a clip finishes and another starts, the selection follows the new clip *if* the
    user was still on the clip that just finished; if they had selected some other clip
    since, it is left alone.
  - When the last clip finishes, the existing behaviour stands: the app goes to Results
    (`_batch_done`), where that clip's own before/after wipe takes over.
  - On failure mid-batch, the selection follows the next clip by the same rule as after a
    success. If the user stays on the failed clip, it shows its decoded recording frame
    under the "paused while cleaning" heading (the batch is still running, so no GPU preview),
    with its "Failed (hover for why)" status; the last snapshot is dropped.
  - On Stop, or on a failure that ends the batch, nothing is running any more, so the clip
    returns to the normal paused before/after preview with its status text, as today.
- **No GPU previews of any clip while a clean runs.** The paused-frame previewer
  (`FramePreviewer`) makes no requests at all while `Runner.busy`, for the cleaning clip
  or any other. Selecting another clip shows its recording frame without the remaster
  (the decoded frame, as the player shows it while playing) and the heading reads
  "Preview · before/after paused while cleaning". Playback of recordings stays (CPU decode).
- Stop stays where it is in the footer; the live view's caption row also gets "Stop" so it is
  next to what you are judging.

### Part 2: flow reuse (behind a switch until it passes the gate)

Today each centre frame t computes RAFT flows to t-3..t+3 (six batched slots,
`flow_iters = 6`), then refines each at full size with LK (`refine_iters = 3`).

Plan:
1. **Profile the whole frame first.** CUDA events around every GPU stage of one frame in
   `enhance`: upload and `prepare` (incl. RAFT encode), RAFT update loop, upsample + LK
   refine, warps, breakup detect + fill, U-Net, remaster stage, packing/download. The total
   should come to ~44.8 ms. **Go/no-go for the flow cache:** build it only if the RAFT
   update loop is ≥ 12 ms/frame (≥ ~25% of the frame), since the cache can at best remove
   two thirds of it. If LK refine, the warps or the remaster stage dominate instead, the
   plan changes to those (fewer refine iterations, refining at half size, the look's
   heaviest pass).
2. **Cheap experiments** (from approach A), each scored with the gate below: `flow_iters`
   6 → 4; repair radius 3 → 2 (the U-Net only reads ±2).
3. **Flow cache.**
   - *When flows are computed (lookahead):* `_windows` already prepares, and RAFT-encodes,
     frame t+3 before centre t is cleaned (`pipeline.py:221`). So when frame k enters the
     window, run RAFT once on the adjacent pair (k-1, k), in both directions:
     F(k-1→k) and F(k→k-1), one batched call with **2 slots**. By the time centre t is
     cleaned, every adjacent-pair flow between t-3 and t+3 is already cached. No change to
     pipeline order, frame buffering or the remaster stage's indexing.
   - *Cache:* keyed by pair, holding RAFT's half-size outputs (the flow images' size,
     before `_upsample` to full size); at most 6 pairs × 2 directions for radius 3. A pair
     is dropped when its older frame leaves the window. That's about 12 × 2 × 478×360 × fp32 ≈ 16 MB of GPU memory.
   - *Chaining*, at half size: `F(t→t+2)(p) = F(t→t+1)(p) + F(t+1→t+2)(p + F(t→t+1)(p))`
     (bilinear sample), and likewise for ±3 and backwards. The result goes through the same
     upsample and full-size LK refine as today, which corrects chaining drift as it corrects
     RAFT's half-size error now.
   - *Ends and cuts:* at the clip's first and last 3 frames `_windows` already yields fewer
     neighbours; the chain covers only the pairs that exist. Each cut part is its own
     `enhance` call, so the cache starts empty per part.
   - *Breakup under flow_reuse (committed):* chained flows are used as they are; no extra
     RAFT slot, so the 2-slot CUDA graph stays fixed. A breakup-damaged t+1 can corrupt
     the chained t→t+2 and t→t+3. The existing repair already weights each neighbour by how
     well it agrees with the centre, so a badly aligned neighbour loses weight rather than
     being copied in. This is *proved, not assumed*: the gate has a breakup-specific test
     (below). If it fails, the fallback is to chain only to ±2 and run RAFT directly for
     ±3 (4 slots, still a fixed graph).
4. **Switch:** `GpuConfig.flow_reuse: bool`, default off, a hidden `--flow-reuse` CLI flag for
   A/B renders.

**Expected speed** (from the 44.8 ms baseline, if step 1 confirms RAFT's update loop at
~2/3 of the 31 ms, ≈ 20.7 ms): the cache runs RAFT on 2 slots instead of 6, cutting that
to ≈ 6.9 ms and saving ≈ 13.8 ms. 44.8 - 13.8 = 31.0 ms/frame ≈ 32.3 fps. A 3-minute flight:
11,032 / 32.3 ≈ 342 s + 9 s start-up ≈ **5.9 min instead of 8.4**. The step 1 profile replaces
this estimate.

**Quality gate** (must pass before `flow_reuse` or any cheap-experiment setting becomes the
default). Every test below runs **with the candidate setting on** (`flow_reuse=True`,
`flow_iters=4`, radius 2, each separately), by parametrising the tests over
`GpuConfig`, not with the defaults:
- The synthetic known-truth tests in `tests/` (fast rolls at 180°/s and 720°/s, breakup
  repair) stay within their current tolerances.
- The approved-look test (`tests/test_remaster.py`, reading `tests/data/approved_look.json`)
  passes.
- A new breakup test: a synthetic clip with a breakup band injected at frame t+1; frame t's
  error against truth with the candidate setting must be no worse than with it off.
- A side-by-side of the owner's fast-roll and breakup-heavy real clips (the
  `a5_v6` set) shows no new smear, ghosting or breakup left behind, judged by eye in
  the app's Results wipe.
- `app/speed.py` constants and the README "Speed" section are re-measured with
  `tools/remaster_speed.py` and updated.

## Open Questions

## Success Criteria

- Within 15 s of pressing Clean, the card names what it is doing; within ~5 s of the
  first progress message, the live wipe shows a cleaned frame. It refreshes every ≤2 s.
- The live view costs <1% fps (measured with `hdz_30s.mp4`, before/after).
- Stop from the live view leaves nothing behind (existing `.partial.mp4` cleanup).
- With `flow_reuse` on and passing the gate: the README's 3-minute flight at default
  settings in ≤6.5 min on the RTX 4070 Ti (today 8.4), with the speed estimate under the
  settings updated to match.

### Amendments accepted into the plan (these override the text above where they differ)

1. **Structure (D1, owner):** no new classes.
   - The snapshot rides `_Writer`'s pinned buffers and CUDA events (`gpu/pipeline.py:1088`).
   - Pair flows and their cache live in `Flow` (`gpu/flow.py`).
   - Profiling and the experiment knobs (`--flow-iters`, `--radius`, `--flow-reuse`, per-stage CUDA-event timing) become flags on `tools/learned_bench.py`.
2. **S1 message dispatch (auto):** `Runner._poll` gets an explicit table of message kinds.
   - `progress`, `frame` and `stage` don't end the job; the newest of each is kept.
   - Only `done` and `failed` end the job, both in the live loop and in the drain loop that runs after the worker exits.
   - When `done` or `failed` arrives in the same poll as kept `frame` or `stage` messages, those are dropped, as pending progress is today (`worker.py:157-166`).
   - The module docstring lists all five kinds.
3. **S2 pair-flow graph (auto):** pair mode captures its own CUDA graph with a different centre per slot. Centre features and contexts are stacked as [f(k-1), f(k)], neighbours as [f(k), f(k-1)]. The existing one-centre graph stays untouched for `flow_reuse=False`.
4. **S3 effects in the gate (auto):** `flows_at` feeds the remaster stage: motion blur reads ±1, and stabilisation reads the flows (`remaster/stage.py:198,254`). So the gate also runs the stabilisation 720°/s flip test (`tests/test_remaster.py:384`) and a motion-blur render, both with the candidate setting on.
5a. **Cheap-experiment release (auto):** a `flow_iters=4` default that passes the gate ships in 0.2.0 with the flow cache, never in 0.1.2. If radius 2 ever wins a repeat blind test, the cache shrinks to 4 pairs × 2 directions and the breakup fallback becomes "run RAFT directly for ±2" (4 slots). The design is otherwise unchanged.
5. **S4 radius stays 3 unless re-blind-tested (auto):** `clean.py:28` records radius 3 as "the 2026-10-12 blind-test winner". Radius 2 is still measured in the experiments, but it only becomes the default after that blind test is repeated. The metric gate alone isn't enough.
6. **A1 frame index under cuts (auto):** the snapshot carries the same running count the progress callback reports (`cli.render`'s `done_before` offset). The caption reads "Frame N of T", where T is the total of kept frames, plus "m:ss into the cleaned clip" (N / fps). The frame count moves off the sketch's heading line into the caption.
7. **A3 first stage message before imports (auto):** the first statement of `work()` sends `("stage", "Starting the GPU")`, before torch is imported, so the 15 s target holds on Windows.
8. **Q2 Stop (auto):** the Stop in the caption row calls the same `_stop_all` as the footer, so it stops the whole batch, as Stop does today.

Factual corrections (no change in behaviour):
- The writer's frames are packed 8-bit I420 (`frame_bytes = w*h + 2*(w/2*h/2)`), so there is no 10-bit path. The writer thread converts the snapshot with `cv2.cvtColor(..., COLOR_YUV2BGR_I420)` and resizes it on the CPU, which sits about 75% idle during a render.
- Raw frames are already numpy arrays in CPU memory, straight from `read_raw`. The raw ring lives in `enhance` and is owned by the main loop: the main loop inserts each raw frame by index as it is prepared, and evicts entries older than (newest index − capacity). Capacity = radius (3, frames the window holds ahead) + the remaster stage's holdback + `_Writer.SLOTS` (6, frames copied but not yet consumed by the writer thread). The writer thread only *reads* from it, under a `threading.Lock` that guards insert, evict and lookup. A missing index (already evicted) skips that snapshot. Each entry is about 2 MB at 956x720.
- `tools/learned_bench.py` only has `--frames` and `--ai` today. The Assignment's runs need amendment 1's flags first.

### Data flow and interaction edge cases

| Interaction | Edge case | Handled? | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean pressed | user has another clip selected | yes | selection moves to the first clip in the batch (lifecycle rule) |
| live view | user selects another clip mid-render | yes | CPU still, "paused while cleaning" heading |
| live view | user reselects the cleaning clip | yes | the latest snapshot shows at once |
| Stop | double-click | yes | `_stop_all` on an idle runner returns at once |
| batch | clip fails mid-batch | yes | selection follows the next clip; the failed clip shows its CPU still |
| batch | 0 clips to clean | yes | Clean is disabled (existing) |
| window | hidden to the tray during a render | yes | snapshots keep arriving and are drawn when the window is shown |
| snapshot | very short clip (< 1.5 s) | yes | the first frame is always sent, then throttled |

Async ordering: a `"frame"` message for clip A could, in principle, arrive after A's `"done"`, if the drain loop races the next clip's start. The rule is that a frame is only drawn for the clip in CLEANING. What guarantees it:
- `Runner` drops A's queue at `_end()` (`self._queue = None`), so nothing from A's queue is read after A ends.
- `start()` makes a new queue for every clip.
- A test enqueues a `frame` after `done` and asserts nothing is drawn.

### Part 1: live view (ships as 0.1.2)

**Messages** (`app/worker.py`, documented in its module docstring):

| Kind | Payload | Terminal? | Poll rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stage` | text | no | newest kept, emitted as `Runner.stage(src, text)` |
| `progress` | done, total, fps, time | no | newest kept (as today) |
| `frame` | before_jpg, after_jpg, clip_index, clip_total | no | newest kept, emitted as `Runner.frame(...)` |
| `done` | as today | yes | kept non-terminal messages from the same poll are dropped |
| `failed` | as today | yes | same |

Unknown kinds are ignored (debug log). The drain loop after the worker exits skips every non-terminal kind.

**Stage messages:** `work()` sends `("stage", "Starting")` first. A `stage` callback is threaded `cli.render → _clean_with_one_retry → _clean → enhance` (and the CPU path), and sends: "Counting frames", "Measuring the picture area and OSD", "Loading the models", "Reading the OSD" (redraw only), "Cleaning" (just before the first frame). The card shows the latest stage until the first `progress` arrives.

**Snapshot.** There is no ring and no cross-thread dict.
- The main loop in `enhance` counts *output frames*, the packed frames handed to `writer.put`: `out_index`, local to this `enhance` call.
- Before `put`, the main thread decides whether this output gets a snapshot: the first output, then any output at least 1.5 s after the last one.
- If yes, it passes `put(packed, snap=raw)`. `raw` is the source frame for that output: the main loop keeps the last `radius + stage holdback + 1` raw frames in a local deque (about 2 MB each, so ≤ ~16 MB), all on the main thread.
- The writer frames are 8-bit I420 (`frame_bytes = w*h + 2*(w/2*h/2)`), with no 10-bit path. The writer thread converts *inside its existing `try`, after `event.synchronize()` and before `free.put(i)` releases the slot*: the pinned I420 buffer goes to BGR (`cv2.COLOR_YUV2BGR_I420`, BT.709 full range, as the encoder tags it). The raw "before" frame goes through the same limited-to-full range conversion `upload()` applies, so the wipe shows no contrast difference that isn't really there. It then resizes both images to 640 px wide, JPEG-encodes them (quality 85), and calls the `snapshot` callback. A `cv2.error` or `ValueError` is logged once and that snapshot is skipped.
- `snap=None` (no raw frame available) means no snapshot.

**Two indices, named.**
- `out_index` is local to one `enhance` call. It picks the raw frame.
- `clip_index` = `done_before + out_index` is the running count across cut parts. It is sent in the `frame` message as `clip_index`, with `clip_total` = the kept-frame total. The caption reads "Frame {clip_index} of {clip_total} · m:ss into the cleaned clip", where m:ss = clip_index / output fps.

**Window.**
- While a clip is CLEANING and selected, `_frame_wipe` shows `show_pair(before, after)`. The heading reads "Cleaning · live: recording / AnaHD; drag the line". The caption is as above, with a Stop button on the caption row that calls the same `_stop_all` as the footer.
- Before is fitted to after's aspect ratio, as the Results wipe does (SuperView, stabilisation zoom).
- **Lifecycle:**
  - On Clean, the runner starts first; then the first batch clip is selected.
  - The selection follows the next clip on done or failed, but only if the user is still on the clip that just ended.
  - On the last clip: Results (existing behaviour).
  - On Stop, or a failure that ends the batch: the paused before/after preview, as today.
  - A clip that failed mid-batch and is still selected: its CPU still (`FrameReader.frame_at`), the "Preview · before/after paused while cleaning" heading and its Failed status.
- **No GPU previews while busy:** `FramePreviewer` gets no requests while `Runner.busy`, and `_preview_ready` discards any result that arrives while busy (one may have been in flight at Clean). Other clips show their CPU still with the "paused while cleaning" heading.
- **Finish time (E1):**
  - Shown in the header and tray tooltip as "done about hh:mm", in the locale's short time format.
  - It appears once the running clip's `Eta` has settled. For each queued clip it adds `speed.py`'s seconds for that clip's kept length × (running clip's `speed.py` fps ÷ its measured fps), so it adapts to the user's GPU. Example: `speed.py` predicts 20 fps and the running clip measures 40 fps, so a queued clip `speed.py` puts at 600 s counts as 300 s.
  - It is recomputed when a clip finishes or fails, and on retry (hidden until the `Eta` settles again). It is removed on Stop and at batch end.

### The gate (relative to today, for any output-changing candidate)

These run under the `gate` pytest marker, through a hidden `anahd --flow-iters N` CLI flag. Each candidate runs **on and off on the same synthetic clips**:
- **PSNR** vs truth: on ≥ off − 0.05 dB.
- **Noise, flicker, breakup-row error** (lower is better): on ≤ off × 1.02. **Fast-roll texture detail** (higher is better): on ≥ off × 0.98.
- **Colour clean-up with default settings:** chroma error vs truth, on ≤ off × 1.02. This consumer reads the cleaner's ±1/±2 flows.
- **Cases:** a breakup band at t+1; a whole-frame outage at t+1; 180°/s and 720°/s rolls; a static scene; OSD over motion.

**Artifact hunt** (amendment 14, the owner's quality bar), on synthetic clips and on every a5_v6 clip:
- **Static-texture pulsing:** in 32×32 px tiles with median flow < 0.25 px and OSD excluded, measure the mean |frame_t − frame_t-1|. The overall mean must be on ≤ off × 1.02, and the 99th-percentile tile on ≤ off × 1.05.
- **OSD box:** for each OSD element, take a 6 px ring outside its mask and compare it with a 6 px band beyond that. The absolute difference in mean luma |ring − band| must be on − off ≤ 1.0 code value, and ring variance on ≤ off × 1.05. The existing `test_osd_pixels_are_not_graded_and_no_box_shows_around_them` must also pass.
- **Difference maps and eye check:**
  - Per-frame |on − off| for every a5_v6 clip.
  - The reviewer opens and looks at: the 20 frames with the largest difference, fixed frames on fast rolls, breakup and night, and temporal strips (one row over 120 frames) for flicker.
  - Any doubt means no default change, and the evidence goes to the owner.
